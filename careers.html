Careers in Project Management

How to break into project management and grow, open roles on our team, and a talent pool where companies find project managers.

How to become a project manager

  1. Start where you are. Volunteer to coordinate a small project in your current role — an event, a process improvement, a system rollout. Most PMs start as "accidental" project managers.
  2. Learn the fundamentals. Work through our free guides and practise with the tools.
  3. Get an entry credential. The Google certificate, CAPM® or PRINCE2® Foundation help get past CV filters. Compare them on our certifications page.
  4. Build a portfolio. Keep anonymised artefacts: a charter, a schedule, a risk register, a status report, a lessons-learned summary. Enter our monthly challenge for portfolio pieces with expert feedback.
  5. Target adjacent roles such as project coordinator, PMO analyst, Scrum Master or delivery associate.
  6. Level up to the PMP® once you have the required experience.

StageTypical titlesFocus

SeniorSenior PM, programme managerMultiple related projects, benefits, senior stakeholders
LeadershipPMO director, portfolio manager, head of deliveryStrategy, governance, capability building

Skills employers ask for

  • Planning and scheduling; risk and issue management; budget control
  • Agile and hybrid delivery
  • Stakeholder management and communication
  • Proficiency with at least one PM platform (see our software guide)
  • Data literacy: dashboards, earned value, forecasting
  • Increasingly: using AI assistants responsibly for summaries, risk spotting and reporting

Salaries vary widely by country, industry and certification. For US figures, see the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook entry for project management specialists.
