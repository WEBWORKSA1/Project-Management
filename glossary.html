Project Management Glossary

Plain-English definitions of the terms you will meet in projects, certifications and job interviews. Type to filter instantly.

ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPQRSTUVW

A

Acceptance criteria
Conditions a deliverable must meet to be accepted by the customer or product owner.
Activity
A distinct, scheduled portion of work performed during a project.
Activity on node (AON)
A network diagramming method in which activities are boxes (nodes) and arrows show dependencies.
Actual cost (AC)
The realised cost of work performed during a given period. Used in earned value management.
Agile
An iterative approach that delivers work in small increments and adapts based on feedback.
Agile Manifesto
The 2001 statement of four values and twelve principles underpinning agile practices.
Analogous estimating
Estimating duration or cost using actual values from a similar past project.
Assumption
A factor considered true for planning purposes without proof. Assumptions should be logged and validated.

B

Backlog
An ordered list of work items waiting to be done.
Backlog refinement
The ongoing activity of adding detail, estimates and order to backlog items.
Baseline
The approved version of the scope, schedule or cost plan, used for comparison against actual performance.
Benefits realisation
Ensuring that the outcomes a project was funded to deliver are actually achieved after handover.
Bottom-up estimating
Estimating each work package individually and aggregating the totals.
Budget at completion (BAC)
The total approved budget for the work to be performed.
Burndown chart
A chart showing work remaining over time, typically within a sprint or release.
Burnup chart
A chart showing work completed over time against total scope, making scope changes visible.
Business case
A justification for a project showing expected benefits, costs, risks and options.

C

Capacity
The amount of work a team or resource can take on in a period.
Change control
A formal process for reviewing, approving or rejecting changes to the baselines.
Change control board (CCB)
A group responsible for reviewing and deciding on change requests.
Change request
A formal proposal to modify scope, schedule, cost or another baseline.
Closing
The process group that formally completes a project or phase.
Communication plan
A document describing who needs what information, when, how and from whom.
Constraint
A limiting factor such as a fixed deadline, budget cap or regulation.
Contingency reserve
Time or money set aside for identified risks (known unknowns).
Cost performance index (CPI)
EV ÷ AC. A measure of cost efficiency; above 1.0 means under budget.
Cost variance (CV)
EV − AC. Negative values indicate a cost overrun.
Crashing
Shortening the schedule by adding resources to critical path activities.
Critical chain
A scheduling method that accounts for resource constraints and uses aggregated buffers.
Critical path
The longest sequence of dependent activities, determining the shortest project duration.
Critical path method (CPM)
A technique for calculating early and late dates and float to find the critical path.
Cumulative flow diagram (CFD)
A stacked area chart showing the number of items in each workflow state over time.
Cycle time
The time an item takes from when work starts on it to when it is finished.

D

DACI
A decision-making framework: Driver, Approver, Contributors, Informed.
Daily Scrum
A 15-minute daily event for Scrum developers to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal.
Decomposition
Breaking deliverables or work into smaller, more manageable parts.
Definition of Done (DoD)
A shared, formal description of the quality standard an increment must meet.
Definition of Ready (DoR)
Criteria a backlog item must meet before a team pulls it into work.
Deliverable
Any unique, verifiable product, result or capability produced to complete a project.
Dependency
A logical relationship between activities, such as finish-to-start.
Duration
The number of work periods needed to complete an activity, excluding holidays.

E

Early finish (EF)
The earliest date an activity can finish, from the forward pass.
Early start (ES)
The earliest date an activity can start, from the forward pass.
Earned value (EV)
The budgeted value of the work actually completed to date.
Earned value management (EVM)
A method integrating scope, schedule and cost to measure performance objectively.
Effort
The number of labour units (e.g. person-hours) needed to complete an activity.
Epic
A large body of work that can be broken down into smaller user stories.
Escalation
Raising an issue or risk to a higher authority when it exceeds the project's tolerance.
Estimate at completion (EAC)
The expected total cost of the project when complete.
Estimate to complete (ETC)
The expected cost to finish all remaining work.
Executing
The process group in which the planned work is carried out.
Expected monetary value (EMV)
Probability multiplied by impact in money terms; used to size contingency.

F

Fast-tracking
Compressing a schedule by performing activities in parallel that were planned in sequence.
Feature
A distinguishing capability of a product that delivers value to users.
Finish-to-finish (FF)
A dependency in which a successor cannot finish until a predecessor finishes.
Finish-to-start (FS)
The most common dependency: a successor cannot start until its predecessor finishes.
Float
The amount of time an activity can be delayed without delaying the project; also called slack.
Focus factor
The proportion of available time a team can spend on planned work after interruptions.
Forward pass
The calculation of early start and early finish dates through a network.
Free float
How long an activity can be delayed without delaying the early start of any successor.
Functional organisation
A structure where staff are grouped by specialty and the functional manager holds authority.

G

Gantt chart
A bar chart showing activities against a timeline, often with dependencies and milestones.
Gate review
A decision point at the end of a phase to continue, change or stop the project.
Gold plating
Adding features beyond what was requested; a form of uncontrolled scope.
Governance
The framework of authority, decision-making and oversight for a project or portfolio.

H

Hybrid approach
A combination of predictive and adaptive practices within one project.

I

Impediment
Anything that blocks or slows a team's progress.
Increment
A usable piece of the product delivered in an iteration.
Initiating
The process group that defines and authorises a new project or phase.
Issue
A current problem or condition that must be resolved; a risk that has occurred.
Issue log
A record of issues, owners, actions and resolution status.
Iteration
A fixed time-box in which a team delivers a usable increment.

K

Kanban
A method for managing flow by visualising work and limiting work in progress.
Kanban board
A visual board with columns representing workflow states and cards for work items.
Kick-off meeting
The meeting that launches a project and aligns the team and stakeholders.
KPI
Key performance indicator: a measurable value showing progress toward an objective.

L

Lag
A delay imposed between a predecessor and successor activity.
Late finish (LF)
The latest date an activity can finish without delaying the project.
Late start (LS)
The latest date an activity can start without delaying the project.
Lead
An acceleration that lets a successor start before its predecessor finishes.
Lean
An approach focused on maximising customer value while minimising waste.
Lessons learned
Knowledge gained during a project, captured to improve future work.
Little's Law
Average cycle time equals average work in progress divided by average throughput.

M

Management reserve
Budget held for unidentified risks (unknown unknowns), outside the cost baseline.
Matrix organisation
A structure in which staff report to both functional and project managers.
Milestone
A significant point or event in a project with zero duration.
Minimum viable product (MVP)
The simplest version of a product that allows learning from real users.
Monitoring and controlling
The process group that tracks, reviews and regulates progress and performance.
Monte Carlo simulation
A technique that runs many random scenarios to model schedule or cost uncertainty.
MoSCoW
A prioritisation method: Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have this time.

N

Net present value (NPV)
The present value of future cash flows minus the investment; used in business cases.
Network diagram
A graphical representation of activities and their dependencies.

O

Objective
A measurable outcome the project is intended to achieve.
OKR
Objectives and Key Results: a goal-setting framework linking ambitious goals to measurable results.
Opportunity
A risk with a potential positive effect on objectives.

P

Parametric estimating
Estimating using a statistical relationship, such as cost per square metre.
PDU
Professional development unit: a measure of learning used to maintain PMI certifications.
PERT
Program Evaluation and Review Technique; uses three-point estimates (O + 4M + P) ÷ 6.
Phase
A collection of logically related activities ending in one or more deliverables.
Planned value (PV)
The authorised budget assigned to work scheduled to be done by a given date.
Planning
The process group that establishes scope, objectives and the course of action.
Planning poker
A consensus-based estimation technique using cards with relative size values.
PMO
Project management office: a function that standardises governance and supports project delivery.
Portfolio
A collection of projects and programmes managed together to achieve strategic objectives.
Precedence diagramming method (PDM)
A technique for building schedule networks using nodes and four dependency types.
Predictive approach
A plan-driven approach where scope, schedule and cost are defined early; often called Waterfall.
Procurement
Obtaining goods or services from outside the project team.
Product Owner
In Scrum, the person accountable for maximising product value and ordering the backlog.
Programme
A group of related projects managed in a coordinated way to obtain benefits.
Progressive elaboration
Iteratively adding detail to a plan as more information becomes available.
Project
A temporary endeavour undertaken to create a unique product, service or result.
Project charter
A document that formally authorises a project and names the project manager.
Project management plan
The document describing how the project will be executed, monitored and closed.
Project manager
The person assigned to lead the team responsible for achieving project objectives.
Project sponsor
The person or group that provides resources and support and is accountable for the business case.

Q

Quality assurance
Activities that give confidence that quality requirements will be met through good process.
Quality control
Inspecting deliverables to verify they meet requirements.

R

RACI matrix
A responsibility chart: Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed.
RAG status
Red, Amber, Green rating used in status reports to show health at a glance.
RAID log
A combined log of Risks, Assumptions, Issues and Dependencies (or Decisions).
Release
A set of increments delivered to users together.
Requirements traceability matrix
A table linking requirements to their origins and the deliverables that satisfy them.
Resource levelling
Adjusting start and finish dates to balance resource demand with supply.
Resource smoothing
Adjusting activities within their float so resource requirements stay within limits without moving the end date.
Retrospective
A meeting where a team reflects on how it worked and agrees improvements.
Risk
An uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, affects at least one objective.
Risk appetite
The degree of uncertainty an organisation is willing to accept for a reward.
Risk owner
The person responsible for monitoring a risk and implementing responses.
Risk register
A document recording identified risks, analysis, owners and responses.
Roadmap
A high-level timeline of planned outcomes, releases or milestones.
Rolling-wave planning
Planning near-term work in detail and later work at a higher level.

S

S-curve
A graph of cumulative cost or work over time, typically shaped like an S.
Schedule performance index (SPI)
EV ÷ PV. Above 1.0 means ahead of schedule.
Schedule variance (SV)
EV − PV. Negative values indicate the project is behind schedule.
Scope
The total of products, services and results to be provided by a project.
Scope creep
Uncontrolled expansion of scope without adjustments to time, cost and resources.
Scope statement
A description of the project scope, deliverables, assumptions and constraints.
Scrum
A lightweight agile framework using sprints, three accountabilities, five events and three artifacts.
Scrum Master
The person accountable for a Scrum team's effectiveness and for coaching Scrum practice.
Scrumban
A hybrid combining Scrum's cadence with Kanban's flow management and WIP limits.
Slack
Another term for float.
SMART objectives
Objectives that are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound.
Sprint
A fixed time-box of one month or less during which a Scrum team creates an increment.
Sprint Goal
The single objective for a sprint, giving the team focus and flexibility.
Sprint Planning
The Scrum event that starts a sprint by defining why, what and how.
Sprint Review
The Scrum event where the team presents results to stakeholders and adapts the backlog.
Stakeholder
An individual, group or organisation that may affect or be affected by a project.
Stakeholder register
A list of stakeholders with their roles, interests, influence and engagement strategy.
Start-to-start (SS)
A dependency in which a successor cannot start until a predecessor starts.
Statement of work (SOW)
A narrative description of products or services to be delivered, often for a contract.
Status report
A periodic summary of progress, risks, issues and decisions needed.
Steering committee
A senior group that provides direction and makes key decisions for a project.
Story points
A relative unit for estimating effort, complexity and uncertainty of backlog items.
Swimlane
A horizontal row on a board used to separate types or classes of work.

T

Task
A unit of work, usually smaller than an activity, assigned to one person.
Three-point estimating
Estimating using optimistic, most likely and pessimistic values.
Throughput
The number of work items completed per unit of time.
Time-box
A fixed, maximum period of time for an activity or event.
To-complete performance index (TCPI)
The cost efficiency needed on remaining work to meet a budget target.
Total float
How long an activity can be delayed without delaying the project finish (LS − ES).
Triple constraint
The interdependence of scope, time and cost; changing one affects the others.

U

User story
A short description of a feature from the user's perspective: As a…, I want…, so that….

V

Variance at completion (VAC)
BAC − EAC. The projected budget surplus or deficit at completion.
Velocity
The amount of work, usually in story points, a team completes per iteration.

W

WBS dictionary
A document detailing each WBS element's description, owner, criteria and estimates.
WIP limit
A cap on the number of items allowed in a workflow stage at one time.
Work breakdown structure (WBS)
A hierarchical decomposition of the total project scope into deliverables and work packages.
Work in progress (WIP)
Items that have been started but not finished.
Work package
The lowest level of a WBS, which can be estimated, scheduled and assigned.
Workaround
An unplanned response to a risk that has occurred and had no planned response.
